Bowe's Interactive Experience Review

The Advent Calendar

INTRODUCTION

This web portfolio is a Self-Initiated Project, developed by myoungaekim. It uses the idea of an Advent calendar, with numbered boxes that users can click to discover hidden Christmas gifts. Instead of showing everything at once, the website encourages users to explore and reveal information, which makes it feel more interactive and engaging.

01 — FIRST IMPRESSIONS

Q1. What was the first thing you paid attention to when interacting with the experience?

When I started to view the webpage, the first thing I noticed was the large numbered grid on the left side of the page. It looked quite unique and special compared to a normal portfolio website. The numbers and empty boxes have made me curious; I realize there might be something hidden behind the grid.

Q2. Spend two minutes with the experience and create a list of each of your discrete actions.

During the two-minute interaction, firstly, I looked at the introduction on the top page, then quickly scrolled down to see the overall content and layout. Meanwhile, I noticed the numbered grid on the left side, so I tried to click the boxes and viewed all the small Christmas gifts that appeared.

After that, I scrolled through the webpage and continued exploring different parts of the website. I discovered and clicked all different project links, such as Ways of Reading 02: A Day in Hyde Park and Butter Shower, to see and read all the information and images.

02 — INTERACTION

Q3. What part of the experience did you spend the most time engaging with?

I spent most of my time exploring and interacting with all the different numbered grids. Each of them showed different gifts, so I wanted to keep clicking and open more boxes to see what I could discover. Also, I spent more time seeing if there were more interactions; I realized I had to double-click the sign to make the gifts disappear. Lastly, I scrolled through the webpage again to see if I missed other content.

Q4. What was the most common action in your two-minute interaction with the experience?

The most common action in my two-minute interaction was clicking. I repeatedly returned to the grid, selected different numbers, and clicked them. Also, during the discovery, I actively tried to click other parts on the webpage, such as the small numbers on the right-hand side and the different links. Clicking was the main way I discovered the website and unlocked new content.

03 — PURPOSE & TIME

Q5. What is your impression of the intended primary goal of the interactive experience?

I think the main goal of the website is to encourage users to explore the portfolio and discover the underlying content themselves, instead of showing all the information obviously.

Q6. How does the interactive experience communicate this primary goal?

For example, during interaction, the Christmas gifts hidden behind the grid enhance users' curiosity to discover. It communicates this goal through the numbered grids and the instruction: Click the boxes to explore; something is hidden until the user clicks a box. This introduces MyoungAe Kim's design work in a unique and interesting way.

Q7. What is your impression of how the experience should be interacted with over time?

I think the website is designed for a short period of free exploration. A user can quickly understand what the website is about, then spend a few minutes looking through all the content. It does not feel like a website that needs to be visited every day, but users can return later to explore sections they missed.

Q8. How does the interactive experience communicate how it should be interacted with over time?

Once the user clicks the numbered grids, they can easily understand and explore all the content quickly. By clicking a grid, the portfolio images at right hand side will automatically switch and show, from number 1 to number 9. This allows users to see all information in a faster way.

04 — MEDIA REFERENCES

Q9. What other media forms does the experience reference?

I think this website also references media forms such as an editorial magazine or design catalogue. As each project combines large images, short descriptions, titles, and project information in a structured layout. This makes the website feel like a digital version of a printed design portfolio magazine.

Q10. What does this reference communicate to you about how you should act when engaging with your research experience?

This reference suggests that I should browse the website slowly, like reading a magazine or design catalogue. I am encouraged to scroll through the projects, look carefully at the images, and read the information that interests me instead of following one fixed path.

Q11. What does this reference communicate to you about how you should feel when engaging with your research experience?

The magazine-like layout makes the experience feel calm and visual. It encourages me to focus on the design work and appreciate the images, typography, and layout. It also makes the website feel professional, like looking through a designer's printed portfolio.

05 — OVERALL EXPERIENCE

Q12. What is the most frustrating part of the interaction to you and what makes that part frustrating?

I think the most frustrating part is that the designer's role in some projects is not always clear, and the website also integrates and contains other projects.

For example, the Butter Shower project lists “Made at Cape” as the client, but it does not clearly explain MyoungAe Kim's specific role in the project. Also, I think there's no clear relationship between other projects and The Advent calendar. This made me unsure and confused about who created the other work. I had to look at the project information more carefully to understand.

Q13. What is the most satisfying part of the interaction to you and what makes that part satisfying?

For me, the most satisfying part is revealing new gifts after clicking a numbered grid. The hidden information creates a small sense of reward and surprise when something new appears. This makes exploring the portfolio more engaging than simply scrolling through a normal list of projects.
